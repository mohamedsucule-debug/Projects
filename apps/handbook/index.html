    How it decides

    1. Reads the question

      Words are reduced to their roots, and different words for the same thing meet: holiday, vacation, PTO and annual leave are one idea.

    2. Searches every sentence

      Each sentence is ranked with BM25 — the scoring behind most search engines — with credit for its section and heading, and for being the kind of answer asked for.

    3. Checks it's really about that

      If the question's key word never appears in the handbook, or its key words never appear together, the answer is “the handbook doesn't say”.

    4. Quotes, never paraphrases

      The answer is the handbook's own sentence, with its source. Nothing is written that the documents don't say.

    5. Flags old policies

      A replaced policy is never the answer. If the old one said something different, it says so — people remember the old number.

      Taking it to a real company

      Where the documents live next step

      SharePoint, Confluence or Google Drive feed the same index. Nothing about the search changes — it already takes documents in as headings and text.

      Who can see what next step

      Each document keeps its permissions, and the search only looks at what the person asking could open themselves.

      Knowing what it doesn't know built

      Refusals come with the reason and the nearest thing it does cover, and every refused question lands in the gaps list for the people who write the policies.

      Proving it, every week built

      Three sets of questions, two of them written after the fact. The tests fail if any score drops, and the report recomputes every time this page loads.
