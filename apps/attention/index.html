Attention watching a circuit form

A transformer, and the exact step it learned to copy.

Forty-five thousand parameters, trained from scratch with an automatic differentiation engine written from nothing — no PyTorch, no JAX, no library at all. Every backward pass in it is checked against finite differences before it is trusted.

For a hundred and fifty steps the loss does not move. Then, over about seventy steps, it falls off a cliff. Drag through training below and watch what happens inside one attention head at that moment — it goes from an even smear to a single bright stripe, and the stripe is the algorithm.

One · A task with exactly one trick in it

A sequence of random tokens, where a block of them repeats. The first block is noise — nothing can predict it, and a perfect model still pays full price there. Everything after it is completely determined. The only way to get any of it is to look back: find where the current token appeared before, and say whatever followed it that time.

Two · The cliff

Loss on the vertical, training steps on the horizontal. The upper line is what guessing uniformly costs; the lower one is the best any model could possibly do, given that the first block is genuinely unpredictable. Nothing happens for a long time, and then everything happens at once. That shape is the signature of a circuit that needs more than one piece before any of it pays.

Three · Drag through training

Every square below is one attention head, at the training step you have selected. The grid is which position attends to which: row i is the model standing at token i, and the brightness along that row is where it chose to look. The black upper triangle is causal masking — nothing can see the future.

Four · Why it takes two layers

A single layer of attention cannot do this, and the reason is worth the paragraph. To answer "what followed this token last time" you first need each position to be marked with what came before it — so one head has to write that mark, and a head in a later layer has to read it and match on it. Two pieces, useless apart. That is exactly why the loss sits flat for so long: gradient has to assemble both halves before either one earns anything.

Five · What this is and is not

The autograd engine is autograd.js, the model is model.js, the training loop is train.mjs, and the weights on this page were produced by running it. Nothing is fetched from a service and no key is needed. 20 gradient checks and 48 model tests. Full write-up.
