How I work

I build fast and I am hard to satisfy. With modern tooling a working version exists in minutes, so the job stops being typing and starts being judgement — what is worth building, what is wrong with it, and what it should say. That is where my time goes, and it is the part that never gets easier.

build

Something running beats something planned

I would rather have a thing I can click that is wrong than a document that is right. Getting to a working version fast is the quickest way to find out what I actually wanted — which is reliably not what I wrote down at the start.

review

Looking at it beats testing it

Every screen here was rendered and inspected after each change. That is how the hourglass that leaked sand, the forest fire that burned one tree and gave up, and the island that came out as a featureless green blob all got caught. No test would have flagged any of them — they were all technically working.

write

The words are half the product

“Pour sand. Add water. Then set it on fire” teaches the sandbox faster than any tutorial could. Deciding what a thing says — and what it refuses to say — is part of building it, not a coat of paint applied at the end.
