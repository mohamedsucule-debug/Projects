How these were made

All eleven were built with Claude Code, an AI that writes and runs code, in a handful of sessions. That is not the interesting part — the interesting part is that AI turns out to be very good at the typing and completely useless at the taste.

01

I describe it, it builds it, I judge it

The loop is: say what the thing should feel like, get a working version in minutes, then look at it with fresh eyes and say what is wrong. Most of my time goes on that last part, because it is the only part a machine cannot do for me.

02

Looking at it beats testing it

Every screen here was rendered and inspected after each change. That is how the hourglass that leaked sand, the forest fire that burned one tree and gave up, and the island that came out as a featureless green blob all got caught. No test would have flagged any of them — they were all technically working.

03

The words are half the product

“Pour sand. Add water. Then set it on fire” teaches the sandbox faster than any tutorial could. Deciding what a thing says, and what it refuses to say, is design work — and it is the work I keep for myself.

Same approach, harder subjects. Each one takes something normally invisible — what a database is thinking, why servers disagree, where a slow web page actually spends its time — and puts it on screen so you can poke it. Fair warning: these ones assume you write software.
